2. The Threshold Map

From Chapter 3. Three zones, per trigger. There is no universal number — there is only your dog's number.

ZoneWhat it looks likeYour dog's distance
GreenNotices the trigger and genuinely does not care. Ears soft, mouth open, takes treats gently. 
YellowNotices, orients, maybe stiffens slightly — but can still respond to its name. This is where the training happens. 
RedOver threshold: barking, lunging, gone. Your only job here is distance, not training.

4. The 8-Week Plan Tracker

From Chapter 12. A scaffold, not a straitjacket — your dog's pace overrides the calendar every time.

Weeks 1–2 — Foundation & management (no deliberate trigger work yet)

Finish the trigger inventory · lock in 2–3 engineered routes · retire aversive gear, fit the front-clip harness · drill the five home behaviors, 2×5 min/day · goal on walks: zero red-zone events, through management alone.

Weeks 3–4 — Begin the emotional work

Add 3–4 short counterconditioning sessions/week in the yellow zone, at your inventory distance · watch for the first head-whip toward you (the conditioned emotional response forming) · layer in LAT once your dog can think near a trigger · 2–3 decompression walks/week.

Weeks 5–6 — Build fluency

Dog offers the check-in look at yellow-zone distances · close distance ~10% per step, only with body language signing off · introduce BAT setups if you have space and a helper · fold skills into real walks. Expect a rough day — use Chapter 10's recovery protocol; it doesn't erase weeks 1–4.

Weeks 7–8 — Consolidate & generalize

Run skills in more places, slightly busier times, still protecting the yellow zone · re-score the trigger inventory every 2 weeks (notice distance / comes-apart distance / intensity 1–5) · watch for shorter recovery and bigger buffer before distances shrink dramatically. Most dogs continue past week 8 — 6 months is a realistic horizon for a long history.
