1. Coturnix incubation calendar (18 days)

Targets: 99.5°F forced-air (101.5–102.0°F at egg height for still-air), 45–50% humidity through day 14, 65–70% from lockdown. Half a degree or five humidity points off costs measurable hatch rate.

Day 0Pre-warm the incubator for at least 4 hours: 99.5°F (forced-air) or 101.5–102.0°F at egg height (still-air), 50% humidity. Pencil an X on one side of each egg and an O on the other.
Days 1–14Turn at least 3 times a day (automatic turner, or flip X-up / O-up every 8 hours). Hold 45–50% humidity. Check the temperature against a separate 0.1°F digital thermometer — new incubators are commonly 1°F off out of the box, and running hot is the most common beginner mistake.
Day 7Candle in a dark room. Keep eggs with a dark embryo and spider-leg veins. Remove clears (fully translucent) and quitters (blood ring, no embryo) — they rot and can contaminate the rest.
Day 14Candle again. Then lockdown: stop turning, remove the turner cradle, lay the eggs in a single layer on the wire floor, raise humidity to 65–70%. Do not open the incubator again.
Days 15–16Hands off. Opening the lid drops humidity 15–20 points and takes 30–60 minutes to recover; dry membranes glue chicks into the shell.
Day 17Hatch day. First pips appear, then nothing for 6–12 hours while each chick absorbs its yolk, then unzipping. A healthy chick is fully out within 24 hours of its first pip. Do not help a struggling chick.
Day 18Late hatchers finish. Chicks are dry within 12 hours and ready for the brooder at 24–36 hours. Eggs that pipped but have not emerged after 36 hours rarely survive.
Bobwhite noteCoturnix run 17–18 days. Bobwhite take longer (about 23 days); the same turning, candling and lockdown rules apply, shifted later.

2. Brooder checklist (before the chicks arrive)

  • Floor space: at least 4 sq ft for a hatch of 20–40 chicks in week 1, 6 sq ft by end of week 2, 8–10 sq ft by end of week 3 (a 2×4 ft 28-gallon tote works for 20–30 chicks for 14 days).
  • Wire-mesh top from day 0 (½-inch hardware cloth, corners zip-tied). Chicks flutter-jump 6 inches by day 3 and clear an open 18-inch tote by day 5. Never a solid lid — ammonia builds within hours.
  • Floor: paper towels or thin shop towels for the first 3 days so chicks see the feed; pine shavings from day 4. Never cedar (respiratory damage). Never newspaper (splayed leg).
  • Heat: a heat plate ($40–90) is the safe choice. If you must use a heat lamp: metal guard, chain rated 5× the bulb weight, UL-listed ceramic socket.
  • Temperature under the heat source: 95–98°F week 1, then drop 5°F per week (90 / 85 / 80 / 75°F). Measure with a probe at chick height; then watch the chicks — piled and loud = too cold, scattered and panting = too hot.
  • Light: 24 hours on for the first 3 days, then 18 on / 6 off through week 3, then 14 on / 10 off.
  • Feed: 24–30% protein gamebird starter, never chicken chick starter (18–20%). Grind pellets or coarse crumble for the first 3 days. Sprinkle a handful on the paper towel for the first 24 hours, then a chick feeder from day 2. Never let it run empty in week 1.
  • Water: a chick-safe waterer with pebbles or marbles in the trough. Drowning is one of the four big first-week killers (with chilling, escape and pasty butt).
  • Enrichment: a 4-inch twig or a rolled paper ball by day 4. Toe- and feather-pecking starts around day 5–7 in bare brooders.

3. Week-by-week care schedule

Week 195–98°F under the plate · 24h light for days 1–3, then 18/6 · paper-towel floor to day 3, shavings from day 4 · feeder from day 2, never empty · check every chick daily for pasty butt.
Week 290°F · expand to 6 sq ft · 18/6 light · fresh shavings, refill feeder and waterer daily · keep the wire top on.
Week 385°F · expand to 8–10 sq ft · switch light to 14/10 at the end of the week · chicks are feathering; watch for pecking.
Week 480°F · fully feathered birds can leave the brooder · plan the adult cage.
Week 5–675°F or room temperature · move to the adult cage · adult laying-age photoperiod is 14 hours of light.

The four rules that matter most in the first 14 days: half a square foot per chick by day 14, a wire top from day 0, paper towels for the first 72 hours, and a waterer chicks cannot drown in.
