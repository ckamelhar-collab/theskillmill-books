The Van Buyer's Inspection & Reliability Checklist

*A free bonus for readers of* Van Life for Beginners *(Open Road Publishing)*

Your van is the one thing in van life that is both your transportation and your home. When it breaks in a way you can't fix, you don't just lose a car — you lose your bedroom, your kitchen, and your ability to get to work, all in the same afternoon, often far from help. That's why buying the *right* van, and inspecting it *properly*, is the highest-stakes decision you'll make. This guide is the checklist a mechanic friend would walk the lot with you.

The golden rule: *Buy the most boring, well-documented, rust-free van you can afford — not the cheapest one, and not the coolest one.* Reliability is freedom. A breakdown is the fastest way to end the dream.

30-second verdicts

  • Most beginners should look at a Ford Transit first. Tall enough to stand, AWD available used, and — critically — any mechanic in America can fix it. Best balance of livability, capability, and low repair anxiety.
  • Ram ProMaster is the value + easy-build pick. Cheapest of the three, widest box, lowest floor, and the flat vertical walls make a first DIY conversion far simpler. Downside: no AWD/4x4 and some electrical quirks.
  • Mercedes Sprinter is the dream — with a warning. It's the best-driving, longest-lived, only-real-4x4 option, but repairs are expensive and require a specialist. Buy one *only* with full service records and a specialist inspection, or a bad DEF/emissions or injector job can eat your build budget in a week.
  • Used Chevy Express / GMC Savana is the budget hero. Cheap, simple, and you can fix it in a parking lot with hand tools. But you can't stand up without adding an aftermarket high top, and it's thirsty. Perfect for a lean bed-and-storage build or testing whether van life is for you before spending $30k.

Part 2 — The pre-purchase inspection checklist (print this)

Bring: a bright flashlight, a magnet (wrap it in cloth — a factory-quality panel holds a magnet; thick body filler over rust won't), a creeper or knee pad to get under it, an OBD2 scanner (~$25) or a phone app, and your reading glasses for the paperwork. Inspect in daylight, never in the rain (water hides scratches and rust and makes everything look clean).

Before you drive there

  • [ ] Run the VIN through Carfax/AutoCheck: check for a salvage/rebuilt/branded title, flood history, accident history, odometer rollback, and open recalls.
  • [ ] Insist the van is cold when you arrive. Tell the seller *not* to warm it up. A pre-warmed engine can hide hard-starting, smoke, and noise. If they "just ran it to the store," come back another day.

Body, frame & rust *(the #1 walk-away issue)*

  • [ ] Get underneath. Inspect the frame rails, crossmembers, rocker panels, floor pan, and around the rear wheel wells. Surface rust is normal; flaky, scaly, or hole-through rust on structural metal is a walk-away.
  • [ ] Press/tap suspect spots. Crunchy or soft metal = rot underneath. Use the magnet on lower body panels — spots it won't stick to may be filler over rust.
  • [ ] Check roof seams, around every window, and the tops of the doors for rust and prior leaks — a leaky roof will ruin your build and your bedding.
  • [ ] Northern/"rust-belt" vans (road salt) rust from underneath; Sun-Belt vans (AZ, NM, TX, CA, FL) are usually far cleaner. A slightly pricier Southern van can be the cheaper van.

Engine & transmission

  • [ ] Cold-start it yourself. Listen for knocking, ticking, or a long crank. Watch the first puff of exhaust: blue = burning oil, white/sweet-smelling = coolant (possible head gasket), black = running rich.
  • [ ] Pull the oil dipstick. Clean amber-to-brown = good. Milky/foamy tan = coolant in the oil = potential blown head gasket or cracked block = walk away.
  • [ ] Check coolant (no oil sheen, correct color, not rusty) and look for leaks, wet spots, or crusty residue on the ground and around hoses.
  • [ ] Diesel Sprinter only: ask specifically about the DEF/AdBlue and emissions system, glow plugs, turbo, and injectors — and confirm it has never been "DPF/emissions deleted" (illegal, won't pass inspection, ruinously expensive to make right).
  • [ ] Automatic transmission: fluid should be pink/red, not brown or burnt-smelling. On the drive, watch for slipping, flaring RPM, or a hard/delayed engagement into Drive or Reverse.

Mileage, history & tires

  • [ ] Mileage in context: diesel Sprinters can run 250,000–300,000+ miles with care; gas Transit/ProMaster/Express are realistic to 150,000–250,000. High miles with full records beat low miles with none.
  • [ ] Demand the service history — oil changes, transmission service, timing/belt, brakes. No records on a diesel is a red flag.
  • [ ] Ex-fleet reality: many cheap vans are ex-Amazon/FedEx/rental. High highway miles are gentle; heavy stop-and-go delivery miles + idling are hard. Ask what the van did for a living.
  • [ ] Tires: read the DOT date code (last 4 digits = week/year of manufacture). Tires over ~6 years old are unsafe even with tread. Check for even wear (uneven = alignment/suspension issues) and that all match. A full set of van tires is $1,000+.

The test drive (at least 20 minutes, including highway)

  • [ ] Cold start → idle → highway → hills → hard brake → reverse. Cover all of it.
  • [ ] Straight-line tracking: hands light on the wheel at 55–65 mph — does it wander or pull? Feel for vibration (tires/driveshaft/wheel bearings).
  • [ ] Brakes: firm pedal, stops straight, no pulsing (warped rotors) or grinding.
  • [ ] Under load: climb a hill or an on-ramp hard — listen for turbo whine, hesitation, or clunks. Try a tight U-turn to hear front-end (CV/joint) noise, especially on the FWD ProMaster.
  • [ ] Everything electric: AC and heat (both — you'll live in this), all windows/locks, wipers, cruise, backup camera, warning lights. Scan for stored/pending trouble codes with your OBD2 — a light cleared right before sale still leaves pending codes.

Before money changes hands

  • [ ] Pay for a Pre-Purchase Inspection (PPI). A shop charges ~$100–$200 to put it on a lift and tell you what's wrong. For a Sprinter, use an actual diesel/Sprinter specialist. This is the single best money you will spend — it either saves you thousands or buys you peace of mind.
  • [ ] Confirm the title is clean and in the seller's name, no liens, VIN on the dash/door-jamb matches the paperwork.
  • [ ] If the seller refuses a cold start, a PPI, or the test drive — walk away. An honest seller has nothing to hide.

Part 4 — Red flags: when to just walk away

No van is worth these. There is always another van. Walk if you see:

  • Structural/frame rust — scaly, flaking, or holed metal on frame rails, rockers, or floor (surface dusting is fine; rot is not).
  • Milky/foamy oil or coolant — the tell-tale of coolant intrusion / head-gasket / cracked block.
  • Overheating on the test drive — temperature climbing past normal, especially on a hill.
  • Transmission slipping, flaring, or clunking into gear.
  • A diesel with no service records, or one that's been DPF/emissions "deleted" (illegal, un-passable, expensive to reverse).
  • Salvage, rebuilt, flood, or branded title — walk unless you personally know exactly what was repaired and why (and even then, insure and finance headaches follow).
  • Seller won't allow a cold start, a real test drive, or a PPI — assume they're hiding something.
  • A check-engine light cleared right before the sale, or fresh pending codes on your scanner.
  • Flood signs — musty/mildew smell, water lines, silt in odd crevices, rusty screws inside, foggy lights.
  • Odometer that doesn't match the wear — worn pedals/seat/steering wheel on a "low-mile" van.
  • A price that's too good to be true. On vans, it always is — it's hiding a transmission, a title, or a rot problem.

*Prices, model availability, and known problem areas shift year to year and van to van. Use this as your framework and your 2026 baseline, then confirm the specific vehicle with your own eyes, an OBD2 scan, a full test drive, and — above all — a professional pre-purchase inspection. Spending $150 on a PPI to avoid a $6,000 transmission surprise is the best trade in van life.*

Buy boring. Buy documented. Buy rust-free. Then go build your home.
