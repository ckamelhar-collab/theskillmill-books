Van Conversion Build-Cost Calculator

Your Free Bonus from *Van Life for Beginners*

Every van build article online gives you the same useless answer: *"anywhere from $5,000 to $50,000."* That's not a budget — it's a shrug.

This worksheet is different. Below is an itemized, fill-in-the-number calculator with real 2026 US prices, broken down by system, at three honest build levels. Price out *your* van, write your numbers in the blank column, add it up, and see exactly how many months of saving stand between you and the road.

How to use it:

  1. For each of the 10 systems, read the three example columns (Shoestring / Mid / Premium).
  2. Decide which tier fits *that system* — you can mix (a Shoestring kitchen with a Mid electrical system is totally normal).
  3. Write your number in the Your Build column.
  4. Copy your system totals into the Summary table at the end, add them up, and use the Savings & Timeline section to get your launch date.

Prices are 2026 US estimates for parts and materials only (DIY labor = free — that's the whole point). All three example columns assume you do the work yourself.

The Three Build Tiers at a Glance

TierWhat it feels likeTarget total (with van)
ShoestringWarm, safe, legal, minimal. Bucket toilet, one fan, cook one pot at a time. Many full-timers start here.~$5,000
MidThe sweet spot. Real fridge, running water, comfortable bed, works in most climates.~$15,000
PremiumA genuine tiny home. Big battery bank, hot shower, induction cooktop, finished cabinetry.~$30,000+

> The single biggest lever: the van itself is 55–65% of your total. If you already own a suitable vehicle, delete the van line and your build drops to $2,100 / $5,680 / $12,250. That's how most beginners actually afford this — buy or use a van first, then build it out while living in it on weekends.

1. Van Purchase

The one thing you can't DIY your way around. Shoestring = an older high-mileage cargo van (think 2003–2011 Chevy Express / Ford E-series, 150k–200k miles). Mid = a newer used van (2013–2017 Transit / ProMaster, ~100k–150k miles). Premium = a low-mileage used Sprinter or Transit.

> DIY or pro? The purchase is all you — but always pay a mechanic $100–150 for a pre-purchase inspection before you buy. A rusted frame, a slipping transmission, or a tired engine can erase your entire build budget in one repair. Rust is the silent killer; walk away from a van with frame or floor rot no matter how cheap.

> DIY or pro? Wire the 12V DC side yourself by following the diagram in Bonus #1 — The Van Wiring Pack. With a correctly-sized fuse on every positive wire and the layout in that pack, this is beginner-safe and will save you $1,000+. Hire an auto-electrician for two things only if you're unsure: the DC-DC tie-in to your starter battery, and any 120V AC shore-power inlet. Bad wiring is the number-one cause of van fires — never guess on wire gauge; the Wiring Pack sizes it for you.

Savings & Timeline — When Can You Actually Go?

Now the whole point: turn your total into a launch date.

The formula:

> Months to launch = (Your grand total − Cash you have saved now) ÷ What you can save per month

Worked examples (starting from $0 saved):

You can save…Shoestring ($5,000)Mid ($15,180)Premium ($34,250)
$200 / mo~25 months (~2 yrs)~76 months~171 months
$400 / mo~13 months~38 months~86 months
$600 / mo~9 months~26 months~57 months
$1,000 / mo~5 months~16 months~35 months

Two moves that collapse those timelines:

  • Already own a van? Subtract it. A Shoestring build on a van you already have is just ~$2,100 — roughly 4 months at $500/mo, not two years.
  • Build incrementally. Buy the van first (the big chunk), get it road-legal and sleepable for a few hundred dollars, move in, and finish the build on weekends while you're *already* saving on rent. Most beginners never save the full $15k in a lump — they roll rent savings straight into the build.
